## The problem
- People in my life struggle with planning because they have trouble visualizing the possibilities of what might happen
- Impact area: Families and neighbors
- Why it matters (impact): People can focus on coming up with more plans and use the AI to gauge if it's a good idea or not based on the simulations.
- Evidence it’s real: The number: In a study of 37 university students finishing an honors thesis, students predicted they would finish in 33.9 days on average. They actually took 55.5 days — about 22 days longer. Only 29.7% finished by the date they predicted.

Even more striking: When asked to predict when they would finish “if everything went as poorly as it possibly could,” fewer than half (48.7%) still finished by that date. In other words, even their worst-case imagination was too optimistic.

A second study in the same paper found people finished only 37.1% of academic projects and 42.5% of nonacademic projects within the time they predicted.

What this means in simple words: People don’t just guess wrong once. They consistently picture a smooth, best-case version of the future and leave out delays, obstacles, and other possibilities. That is exactly the “trouble visualizing what might happen” problem.

Source: Buehler, Griffin & Ross (1994), “Exploring the Planning Fallacy: Why People Underestimate Their Task Completion Times,” Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. The PDF is hosted by MIT.

Website: Massachusetts Institute of Technology (web.mit.edu)

Exact link: https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/biases/67_J_Personality_and_Social_Psychology_366,_1994.pdf
